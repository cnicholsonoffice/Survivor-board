Why LAC, and why now

The no-reuse rule makes every team a single-use asset, so the question is never just "will they win" — it is "is this the week to spend them". Each number below is a counterfactual: the model re-solves the whole season under a different assumption and reports what changed.

Is this their best use?
This is LAC’s best slot of the whole season. Spending them anywhere else is worse for your season, so there is nothing to save them for.
What saving them costs
Hold LAC back and you would use JAX this week at 80% and push LAC to Week 11 (78%) — that is 9% of your season equity gone to keep an option you never get a better chance to use.
How replaceable they are
Losing LAC entirely would cost 16% of your season. They clear 65% in 3 of the 20 remaining legs — a specialist, so the window matters.
Win probability vs. season value
This is also their highest win probability of the season (81%), so the two views agree.

How much to trust this

Where a moneyline is posted, the win probability is that price with the bookmaker's margin stripped out by Shin's method, which pulls more of the juice off the longshot than a simple proportional de-vig and so lands closer to the truth on heavy favourites. A moneyline pushes on a tie, so the de-vigged number is already "wins, given no tie" — the tie is then priced back in separately at 0.28%, because in Circa a tie eliminates you. Where only a spread is posted, it is converted through an empirical margin model fit on 1,088+ games of closing lines (margin sd 13.2, held-out log loss 0.6238). Games with neither are projected from ridge-fit power ratings (home field 1.81, scale correction ×1.177) and pulled toward a coin flip in proportion to how far out they are.

These are not Circa's prices. Odds come from the nflverse feed, which is compiled consensus pricing across books. Circa publishes no public feed, so the numbers here track Circa closely but are not identical to what you will see on your ticket — check the actual price before you submit.

Where the uncertainty band comes from

The plan is scored 0.200% on average across simulations that jitter every team's rating and hold the plan fixed — the honest number. Letting each simulation rebuild its plan after seeing the shock gives 0.223%, which is hindsight and therefore an upper bound. The tile reports the honest one.

What this model does not know

Injuries and QB changes only reach it through the market — a posted line prices them, a projected one does not. Circa pick popularity is not published before the deadline, so the field column is a behavioural model, not observed data. Weather, short weeks and Week 18 resting-starters motivation are not modelled at all; Week 18 is the one leg to override on judgment.

Why LAC, and why now

The no-reuse rule makes every team a single-use asset, so the question is never just "will they win" — it is "is this the week to spend them". Each number below is a counterfactual: the model re-solves the whole season under a different assumption and reports what changed.

Is this their best use?
This is LAC’s best slot of the whole season. Spending them anywhere else is worse for your season, so there is nothing to save them for.
What saving them costs
Hold LAC back and you would use JAX this week at 74% and push LAC to Week 2 (76%) — that is 7% of your season equity gone to keep an option you never get a better chance to use.
How replaceable they are
Losing LAC entirely would cost 18% of your season. They clear 65% in 3 of the 20 remaining legs — a specialist, so the window matters.
Win probability vs. season value
This is also their highest win probability of the season (81%), so the two views agree.

Thanksgiving and Christmas are the whole game. Those two legs have 4 and 3 games, so the candidate pools are tiny and several teams appear in both. Entries die there in December because they spent those teams in September.

Measured: replaying 2021-2026, an entry that just takes the biggest available favorite every week runs out of legal teams before the Christmas leg in two of six seasons — an automatic elimination — and in the seasons it does survive, planning the full route is worth a median 2.25× on the probability of running the table. 2026 is one of the two seasons where greedy picking dies outright.

How much to trust this

Win probabilities come from an empirical margin model fit on 1,087+ games of closing lines: margin sd 13.2, and a tie priced at 0.28% because in Circa a tie eliminates you. Held-out log loss 0.6239. Games with no posted line are projected from ridge-fit power ratings (home field 1.76, scale correction ×1.168) and pulled toward a coin flip in proportion to how far out they are.

Where the uncertainty band comes from

The plan is scored 0.161% on average across simulations that jitter every team's rating and hold the plan fixed — the honest number. Letting each simulation rebuild its plan after seeing the shock gives 0.186%, which is hindsight and therefore an upper bound. The tile reports the honest one.

What this model does not know

Injuries and QB changes only reach it through the market — a posted line prices them, a projected one does not. Circa pick popularity is not published before the deadline, so the field column is a behavioural model, not observed data. Weather, short weeks and Week 18 resting-starters motivation are not modelled at all; Week 18 is the one leg to override on judgment.

Moneyline — the book's direct price on this team winning outright. Where one is posted the model reads win probability straight off it (vig removed), rather than translating a spread. ML means priced that way; spread means no moneyline yet; proj means the game is unpriced and the number comes from power ratings.
Win% — chance this team wins its own game this week, after the vig is stripped out and a tie is priced as a loss.
Season equity if picked — the bar. Your chance of going 20-0 across the whole contest if you spend this team now and play perfectly after. Tiny by nature: the best bar on the board is under 1%.
Kept — that bar as a share of the best bar. 100% means the pick costs you nothing. 85% means choosing it throws away 15% of what your entry is currently worth.
Est. field — modelled guess at what share of the ~14,000 entries take this team. Not published by Circa, so treat it as a rough shape.
EV# — rank once prize-splitting is priced in: winning alongside fewer people is worth more. Green means it ranks better here than on survival alone. It is a tiebreaker, not a headline.

Why LAC, and why now

The no-reuse rule makes every team a single-use asset, so the question is never just "will they win" — it is "is this the week to spend them". Each number below is a counterfactual: the model re-solves the whole season under a different assumption and reports what changed.

Is this their best use?
This is LAC’s best slot of the whole season. Spending them anywhere else is worse for your season, so there is nothing to save them for.
What saving them costs
Hold LAC back and you would use JAX this week at 79% and push LAC to Week 11 (78%) — that is 4% of your season equity gone to keep an option you never get a better chance to use.
How replaceable they are
Losing LAC entirely would cost 16% of your season. They clear 65% in 4 of the 20 remaining legs — flexible, so the timing is less delicate.
Win probability vs. season value
This is also their highest win probability of the season (80%), so the two views agree.

How much to trust this

Win probabilities come from an empirical margin model fit on 1,089+ games of closing lines: margin sd 13.2, and a tie priced at 0.28% because in Circa a tie eliminates you. Held-out log loss 0.6241. Games with no posted line are projected from ridge-fit power ratings (home field 1.81, scale correction ×1.199) and pulled toward a coin flip in proportion to how far out they are.

Where the uncertainty band comes from

The plan is scored 0.192% on average across simulations that jitter every team's rating and hold the plan fixed — the honest number. Letting each simulation rebuild its plan after seeing the shock gives 0.218%, which is hindsight and therefore an upper bound. The tile reports the honest one.

What this model does not know

Injuries and QB changes only reach it through the market — a posted line prices them, a projected one does not. Circa pick popularity is not published before the deadline, so the field column is a behavioural model, not observed data. Weather, short weeks and Week 18 resting-starters motivation are not modelled at all; Week 18 is the one leg to override on judgment.

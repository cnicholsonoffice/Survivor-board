Win% — chance this team wins its own game this week, taken from the moneyline with the bookmaker's margin removed. A row is only tagged when it came from somewhere else: spread means no moneyline was posted so the points line was converted instead, proj means nothing is posted yet and it is projected from power ratings.
Spread — the posted points line, shown for reference. It no longer drives the maths when a moneyline exists.
Season equity if picked — the bar. Your chance of going 20-0 across the whole contest if you spend this team now and play perfectly after. Tiny by nature: the best bar on the board is under 1%.
Kept — that bar as a share of the best bar. 100% means the pick costs you nothing. 85% means choosing it throws away 15% of what your entry is currently worth.
Usable — how many of the remaining legs this team is still a genuine option in, counting every leg where it is at least 65% to win. A 1 is a warning: spend them now or you will probably never get to spend them at all. A high number means they keep, so there is no rush.
Est. field — modelled guess at what share of the ~24,900 live entries take this team. Circa does not publish this before the deadline, but it does publish the result afterwards, and the estimate is now fitted to the Week 1 selections rather than guessed. Still a rough shape, not a number to lean on hard.
EV# — rank once prize-splitting is priced in: winning alongside fewer people is worth more. Green means it ranks better here than on survival alone. It is a tiebreaker, not a headline.
# — rank out of all 30 teams playing this leg, best season outcome first. Every team is listed; both sides of a matchup appear, since either one is a legal pick.

Why KC, and why now

The no-reuse rule makes every team a single-use asset, so the question is never just "will they win" — it is "is this the week to spend them". Each number below is a counterfactual: the model re-solves the whole season under a different assumption and reports what changed.

Is this their best use?
This is KC’s best slot of the whole season. Spending them anywhere else is worse for your season, so there is nothing to save them for.
What saving them costs
Hold KC back and you would use SEA this week at 75% and push KC to Week 9 (76%) — that is 12% of your season equity gone to keep an option you never get a better chance to use.
How replaceable they are
Losing KC entirely would cost 25% of your season. They clear 65% in 7 of the 18 remaining legs — flexible, so the timing is less delicate.
Win probability vs. season value
This is also their highest win probability of the season (84%), so the two views agree.

How much to trust this

Where a moneyline is posted, the win probability is that price with the bookmaker's margin stripped out by Shin's method, which pulls more of the juice off the longshot than a simple proportional de-vig and so lands closer to the truth on heavy favourites. A moneyline pushes on a tie, so the de-vigged number is already "wins, given no tie" — the tie is then priced back in separately at 0.28%, because in Circa a tie eliminates you. Where only a spread is posted, it is converted through an empirical margin model fit on 1,119+ games of closing lines (margin sd 13.2, held-out log loss 0.6242). Games with neither are projected from ridge-fit power ratings (home field 1.07, scale correction ×1.071) and pulled toward a coin flip in proportion to how far out they are.

These are not Circa's prices. Odds come from the nflverse feed, which is compiled consensus pricing across books. Circa publishes no public feed, so the numbers here track Circa closely but are not identical to what you will see on your ticket — check the actual price before you submit.

Where the uncertainty band comes from

The plan is scored 0.224% on average across simulations that jitter every team's rating and hold the plan fixed — the honest number. Letting each simulation rebuild its plan after seeing the shock gives 0.256%, which is hindsight and therefore an upper bound. The tile reports the honest one.

What this model does not know

Injuries and QB changes only reach it through the market — a posted line prices them, a projected one does not. Circa pick popularity is not published before the deadline, so the field column is a behavioural model, not observed data. Weather, short weeks and Week 18 resting-starters motivation are not modelled at all; Week 18 is the one leg to override on judgment.
